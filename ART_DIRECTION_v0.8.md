# TenAce 0.8 art direction

The user's supplied tennis-game reference guided the illustrated club atmosphere, navy/green interface, blue court, portrait cards, and circular shot controls. Its phone frame and decorative score values were not copied into gameplay.

## Scenery asset

Saved project asset: `assets/club-scenery-v08.png` (1536 × 1024 PNG).

Generated with the built-in image-generation tool using the supplied image as a style reference. The scenery is separate from interactive court geometry, players, net, and ball. If the image cannot load, the procedural environment remains available. Solstice applies a warm tint and retains its terracotta court.

Final generation prompt:

> Use case: stylized-concept. Create a production game BACKGROUND SCENERY asset, landscape 1536x1024. Image 1 is STYLE REFERENCE ONLY: match its richly illustrated sports game aesthetic, dark navy and fresh green palette, detailed foliage, sunlit upscale public tennis club, crisp painted comic realism. Show a symmetrical clubhouse with glass windows in the middle distance, lush trees framing both sides, blue sky and white clouds, a row of small spectators behind a dark navy mesh windscreen fence across the full bottom third. Camera looks straight toward back fence from a tennis court. Bottom edge is a narrow strip of green grass. No playing court, no court lines, no tennis net, no foreground players. No phone frame, no UI, no logos, no words or letters. This image is the distant environment behind an independently rendered interactive court; focus on beautiful layered architectural and foliage detail, sun/shadow contrast. Fill the image edge to edge.

## Animated characters

The character rig is drawn in code so existing skin, hairstyle, clothing, racket color, and racket shape remain customizable. Longer limbs, bent knees, outlined silhouettes, clothing folds, headbands, racket strings, and front/back poses move the style toward the reference. These figures remain simpler than the detailed painted characters in the supplied concept.

The artwork does not change progression or tennis rules. Scores still use the prototype's first-to-seven format.
